About the Thinking Lab Collection

The Thinking Lab Collection turns two print activities from the close of Book 1 into interactive labs. Where the other Discovery-Zone collections let you test a single physical idea, these two labs exercise how you think about all of them together.

The Wheelwork Map — synthesis

A concept map where every Book 1 idea is a gear and every line shows which gear turns which. The point is not to memorize a diagram but to see that separate discoveries — counting, ratio, spiral, wave, beat, force, the three phases — are one connected machine whose center is the balanced beat, 1/x = 1 + x.

The Question Wall — inquiry

A place to post the questions the book opened rather than closed. Curiosity is the engine that keeps the wheelwork turning: every idea you truly understand shows you a new door. Sort wonderings into five columns, turn any wonder into a testable plan, and carry your best question into Book Two.

User Guide

Wheelwork Map

  • Tap any gear to see what it is, the hands-on apparatus Sophie used, and the reasoned links to every gear it turns.
  • Selecting a gear dims the rest and lights up its connections. Tap the central hub to reset.
  • Try to trace an unbroken path from counting patterns to the three phases — and notice how each link comes back to balanced beats.

Question Wall

  • Press + Add a question, pick a column, and (optionally) add an “I could test this by…” note that turns a wonder into an experiment.
  • Drag a card between columns, or use the card's move menu. Mark a card Answered when you've tested it — then write the new question it opened.
  • Your wall is saved automatically on this device. Export downloads your cards; Print makes a paper copy.

Teacher Guide

Both labs are synthesis-and-inquiry tools best used after learners have worked several chapters, or as a capstone at the end of Book 1.

With the Wheelwork Map

  • Ask learners to defend one link out loud: why does this gear turn that one? The reason is the learning, not the arrow.
  • Challenge: find the longest chain of connected gears, then show how every step reduces to balanced beats.

With the Question Wall

  • Frame it as exploration, never a test — curiosity is the goal. Model one card of your own first, including an “I could test this by…” plan.
  • Sort as a class; revisit weekly. When a card is answered, insist on the new question it opened — that habit is the whole point.
  • Export or print the wall to carry the best questions forward into Book Two.
