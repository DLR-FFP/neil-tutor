PART 1: THE AXIOMATIC FOUNDATION

1.1 What Is Sankhya?

Sankhya (Sanskrit: counting, number, ratio, axiomatic reasoning) is an axiomatic science
of counted interaction in a real, dynamic, coherent substratum. Created by Maharishi
Kapila in a pre-Vedic period (~32,000 years ago), Sankhya forms the scientific core of
the Bhagavadgita, embedded in the Mahabharata.



  "It is the only unified field theory in existence that offers a complete scientific and
  mathematical solution to every aspect of reality, manifest or unmanifest, in space
  comprising substantial components." — G. Srinivasan, SoS1, Abstract/Overview



Critical distinction: Sankhya is spelled with the anuswara (ascending nasal sound),
meaning "numbers, counting, reasoning." The common spelling "Samkhya" uses a
descending labial sound that classifies it into a different category. We always use
Sankhya per G. Srinivasan's authentic transliteration. (Source: SoS2, Preface, pp. 12–
13)

A rigid bow with a string attached: the outer curve stretches while the inner surface

Neither the bow nor the string changes its state. This is the PHO state.

(Source: SoS1, Overview; SoS2, Preface)

10.3 The Train Analogy (Simultaneous vs. Sequential)

Wagon length L = rigid (simultaneous). Gap G = variable (sequential). Both ends of each
wagon act simultaneously. The gap between wagons is the sequential interval. Changes
in velocity alter the Lt/Gt ratio instantly, requiring a balancing process: this is why 1/x =
1 + x.

(Source: SoS1, pp. 8–9)

Moolaprakriti — are exactly like that porous pot: immersed in a resonant sea of energy.
The more still and coherent we become, the more fully we absorb from the field. The
Siddhi process is the deliberate cultivation of that stillness.
